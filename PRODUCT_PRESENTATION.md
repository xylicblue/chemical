# Product presentation research

Reviewed 9 November 2026 for Tahir Chemicals' enquiry-led sales website.

## Findings

- [Univar's product catalogue](https://www.univarsolutions.com/product-catalog) presents buying information such as product names, grades, packaging, suppliers, documents, and quote actions. The relevant lesson is to make purchasing information easy to scan.
- [IMCD's customer portal](https://imcd.my.site.com/imcdgroup/myimcd) connects product search with pack sizes, documentation, samples, quotations, and sales support. Its account and ordering model is different from this website; the useful pattern is a direct path from product discovery to an enquiry.
- [Nielsen Norman Group's visual hierarchy guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/) explains how contrast, scale, and grouping guide attention. Its guidance supports a restrained palette with clear differences between names, supporting information, and actions.

These observations inform the proposed design. They do not establish that the design will increase sales; that would require observing enquiries and testing with buyers.

## Implemented direction

1. Following client feedback, show names only in a clearly framed product directory on the landing page, with a navy toolbar, numbered product tiles, and 12 visible products per page. A compact name-search field sits beside the count on the right. Keep the client's Studio order.
2. Open a single-product modal for the description, grade, packaging, procurement specifications, and documents, respecting the client's field visibility choices. Do not use decorative formulas or large technical identifiers.
3. Use pale blue directory items, navy product detail headers, pale sage supply panels, and clearly contrasted quote buttons.
4. Preserve the existing enquiry form, which is prefilled with the selected product. Keep secondary details available without overwhelming the catalogue.
5. Preserve client content and field visibility choices. Do not add stock, price, certification, or delivery promises without confirmation.

Client-approved photographs of actual products or packaging could be added later. Generic packaging photographs would not establish the actual appearance, pack size, manufacturer, or stock position, so the current design uses typography and color without implying those facts. No content, images, or code from the researched sites was copied into the website.
